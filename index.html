# ex-01 prompts

Engine: Z-Image Turbo via the authenticated Hugging Face MCP (mcp-tools/Z-Image-Turbo),
1024x1024, 8 steps, shift 3, fixed seeds. Text only: this engine has no image-input
slot and no negative prompt, so every style card was bound verbally, never as pixels.

The local node was restored and both local engines were then run against the same
locked prompt. Neither could match: FLUX.1-dev Q4 reproduces the geometry exactly but
renders every style as the same flat glossy cartoon, and SDXL renders real oil paint
but cannot hold the geometry spec even at 77 tokens, with or without IP-Adapter. Full
comparison table and candidate files in NOTES.md. That is why the finals are Z-Image.

Prompt structure, identical in all five runs:

    <locked crown paragraph>
    Style: <the only part that changes>
    <locked background/control paragraph>

## Locked crown sentence (identical in every style)

A gold king's crown standing alone. One plain gold band with exactly five tall narrow
triangular points rising from it, each point bare smooth gold and ending in a small
round gold ball. A small plain gold cross stands upright on top of the middle point.
One large oval crimson ruby is set at the front centre of the band, with two round
navy sapphires on the band to its left and two round navy sapphires to its right.
The crown is open and empty at the top: no cap, no lining, no wearer, no head.
Front view at eye level, the crown centred with generous empty space all around it.

## Locked control paragraph (identical in every style)

Flat single-colour cream background covering the whole picture to all four edges.
No shadow, no text, no frame.

## The five style blocks

01 toy-box  (winner: seed 1101)
Style: toy-box illustration, simple geometric shapes, bold primary colours, thick
even outlines, cheerful graphic like wooden toys. Palette of cherry red, sunflower
yellow, sky blue, leaf green and warm wood-tan, flat fills.

06 power-card  (winner: inkC seed 3303)
Style: 1960s screen-printed poster art, cel-shaded. The gold is drawn as flat printed
ink, not shiny metal: two solid tones of mustard and ochre only, no gloss, no
highlights, no gradients. Every shape is enclosed by a bold uneven charcoal ink
outline, with visible halftone paper grain over the flat colour. Palette strictly
mustard gold, cream, charcoal ink, crimson and navy.

Two weaker style blocks were tried first and are kept as candidates: the plain
"mid-century modern / flat spot colours / thick tapered contour lines" wording
(lock2-seed1101, lock2-seed2202) rendered glossy metal with no ink line, and
adding "no gradients / bold uniform outline" (lock2-inkB-seed1101) only half fixed
it. The winner is the version that attacks the material directly - "the gold is
drawn as flat printed ink, not shiny metal".

08 bedtime-story  (winner: seed 2202)
Style: bedtime-story watercolour, soft wet-on-wet brushstrokes, warm glowing paper,
gentle soft edges, delicate pigment pooling. Palette of honey gold, peach, dusty
rose, sage and cream.

11 emperor's-clothes  (winner: seed 2202)
Style: painted in classical oil paint, visible oil brushwork, glazed layered pigment,
soft modelled highlights on the metal, warm gallery light, the surface quality of an
old-master still-life study on canvas.

12 the-die  (winner: seed 1101)
Style: flat vector minimalism, precise geometric shapes, hard edges, hairline outline,
large unmodulated colour fields, elegant and exact. Two or three flat colours plus
the gold of the crown.

## What worked / what broke

Worked: rewriting the locked object sentence fixed the one criterion the first pass
failed. Three words did the work. "Five tall pointed arches" became "five tall narrow
triangular points, each ending in a small round gold ball"; "slight three-quarter
view" became "front view at eye level"; and the object paragraph moved ahead of the
style block. The same crown is now readable in all five styles, including the oil
painting that previously produced a fleur-de-lis coronet.

Broke: describing style purely as a school or period is not enough when the material
has a strong prior of its own - 06 kept rendering glossy metal until the prompt said
what the gold is made of. Subject-first ordering also costs some style strength: the
first pass, with the style block leading, gave a heavier toy-box outline. Z-Image has
no negative prompt, so "no shadow" is only a suggestion: 06, 08 and 11 still carry a
soft cast shadow. It sits on the flat cream field and lifts with the background, but
on a real job the shadow would be removed at cutout.

הערה זמנים: SDXL יציב ~40 שניות/תמונה ב-1024. FLUX Q4 ריצה ראשונה **270 שניות** ל-1024/20 steps (כולל המתנה בתור). Z-Image דרך MCP ~20 שניות.

ex-02 - product photo -> styled game component + cutout
=======================================================

ENGINE: SDXL 1.0 img2img on the G5 (192.168.0.27), local, no cloud.
CUTOUT: BiRefNet_HR on the same box, via skills/scripts/comfy_cutout.py.

THE ONE DECISION THAT MADE THIS WORK
    Identity comes from the photo's pixels, style comes from the words.
    The photo is the starting latent (img2img at denoise 0.80-0.82); the style
    is text only. Every attempt to move either job to the other channel failed -
    see "what broke" below.


LOCKED STYLE BLOCK (identical tail on all three objects)
--------------------------------------------------------
Bold black ink outline, thick clean line art, flat two-tone cel shading,
simplified shapes, no photographic detail. Flat plain white background,
no shadow, no text.

For the pouches only, the outline clause was strengthened to
"Heavy uniform black contour line around each pouch, bold thick clean line art"
in a later pass - it did not win. See what broke.


SUBJECT LINES (the only part that changes per object)
-----------------------------------------------------
dice
    Cel-shaded vector game illustration of black marbled dice with bright
    yellow round pips.
    + locked style block

crown
    Cel-shaded vector game illustration of a small antique brass crown ring:
    a short round band with ball-tipped triangular points rising around the
    top, and an engraved zigzag pattern along the band. Dark weathered bronze
    with worn gold highlights.
    + locked style block

pouches
    Cel-shaded vector game illustration of three velvet drawstring pouches
    side by side: one dark teal green, one light grey, one dusty purple, each
    gathered at the top with a cord and a small black toggle.
    + locked style block


NEGATIVE (shared, _runner/negative.txt)
---------------------------------------
text, letters, words, numerals, caption, signature, watermark, logo, border,
picture frame, matting, photorealistic, photograph, 3d render, blurry, low
resolution, jpeg artifacts, cropped, cut off, head, person, hands, cast shadow,
contact shadow, drop shadow, gradient background, vignette, background scenery,
props, clutter, table, surface, reflection, glossy highlight, specular,
realistic material, soft airbrush shading

      --out-dir outputs/ex-02 --trim --cut-interior-white


PHOTO PREP (pouches only)
-------------------------
The pouches photo carries printed sizes ("14x18cm/16x20cm/20x30cm") across the
top. At any denoise that text survives as fake lettering, and the negative
prompt does not remove what the init latent puts there. So the photo is cropped
to the pouches, its near-neutral studio backdrop is forced to pure white, and
the crop is centred on a 1024 white canvas before it is used as the init image.
Crop the text out; do not argue with it in the prompt.


WHAT WORKED
-----------
1. Denoise is the identity/style dial, and the usable window is narrow.
   0.60 = still a photograph with vector pips. 0.75 = a filtered photograph.
   0.80-0.82 = a drawing that is still unmistakably the photographed object.
   0.88 = the drawing starts inventing dice.
2. Style by text held up. SDXL took "bold black ink outline, flat two-tone cel
   shading" without any reference image, which is the same thing ex-01 found
   for oil paint. Words are enough for a rendering language.
3. One locked style block across three very different objects gave a set that
   reads as one family - same line weight, same two-tone shading, same white
   field.
4. The white generation background made the cutout trivial: BiRefNet returned
   clean contours with no halo and no despill needed on any of the three.


WHAT BROKE
----------
5. Style reference + img2img together destroyed the object. Adding the
   06-power-card card as an IP-Adapter reference at only 0.5 turned the black
   marbled dice teal with gold veins and grew a d4 out of the pile. IP-Adapter
   imports the reference's palette, not just its rendering language - the same
   trap as ex-01. Once the style is in the text, do not also hand over a card.
6. IP-Adapter cannot count. The photo bound as an IP reference gave the right
   material but produced four dice for a prompt that asked for two.
7. Text-only identity inverted the object. With no init image, "black marbled
   dice with bright yellow pips" came back as white dice with black pips. The
   photo is doing real work that the words cannot replace.
8. My own negative prompt fought the subject. Adding "yellow die, yellow cube,
   cheese" to stop one die rendering yellow also suppressed the yellow pips and
   punched black holes where pips belonged. A negative term does not know which
   object you meant it for - it suppresses the colour everywhere.
9. Pip count does not survive at scale. Twelve dice means about a hundred pips,
   and SDXL cannot keep them regular; giant pips and wrong faces appeared in
   every pass. This is almost certainly why the production example
   (component-dice-pair-api-1.png) is a PAIR. Fewer components, fewer places to
   be wrong.
10. Pushing outline weight multiplied the objects. The "heavy uniform black
    contour line" pass at cfg 8.5 gave bolder lines and four or five pouches
    instead of three. Identity beat line weight, so the earlier pass won.
11. BiRefNet filled the crown's open middle with backdrop white, exactly as
    README-NOTES warned. It is invisible on a white page and shows up the moment
    the asset is placed. Detecting it needs a check for opaque backdrop-light
    blobs enclosed by artwork, not a check for transparent holes -
    comfy_cutout.py now reports that number on every asset and cuts it with
    --cut-interior-white (54,710 px on the crown).


DEVIATION FROM THE BRIEF, STATED PLAINLY
----------------------------------------
The brief's quality example for the dice photo is a pair on a magenta key. I
kept the photo's full arrangement instead, because the acceptance criterion asks
for the illustrated object to be recognisable as the photographed one down to
the number of dice, and img2img preserves that arrangement for free. The pair
was attempted twice and both routes are in candidates/:
ex-02-dice-pair-ipphoto080-* (wrong count, no outline) and
ex-02-dice-pair-t2i-* (inverted colours). Generation was on white rather than
magenta because BiRefNet is the local path per README-NOTES; the chroma route
needs numpy and scipy, which this laptop does not have.

הערה **Category:** props / img2img. Photo of a real component -> illustrated component

ex-03 - one icon, two registers (flat and rich), three new subjects
===================================================================

ENGINE: SDXL 1.0 on the G5 (192.168.0.27). Cutouts by BiRefNet_HR on the same
box. Contour and framing by code, not by the model - see below.


WHAT SEPARATES flat FROM rich IN THIS FAMILY
--------------------------------------------
Read off the three approved pairs (meat, fish, wheat) before writing anything:

| trait | flat | rich |
|---|---|---|
| silhouette | identical between registers | identical between registers |
| outline | very heavy uniform black, same weight in both | same |
| tones per colour | 2 - a base and one darker shade | 3-5 - base, shadow, bright highlight |
| highlights | none | yes, a clear specular |
| interior detail | large simple fields, few division lines | veins, grain, texture lines |
| saturation | slightly muted | pushed brighter |
| background | flat white, no shadow, no ground | same |

So the pair is not two drawings of a subject. It is one drawing at two render
levels, and only the tone count, highlight and interior detail may move.


THE PROMPTS THAT WON (short on purpose - see finding 26)
-------------------------------------------------------
flat, one per subject:
    Flat vector game icon, one whole crusty oval bread loaf, golden brown.
    Thick heavy black outline, two flat tones only, no highlight, no texture.
    Pure white background.

    Flat vector game icon, one triangular wedge of yellow cheese with round
    holes. Thick heavy black outline, two flat tones only, no highlight, no
    texture. Pure white background.

    Flat vector game icon, one dark green olive fruit with a small leaf on its
    stem. Thick heavy black outline, two flat tones only, no highlight, no
    texture. Pure white background.

rich, one per subject - same subject clause, register clause swapped:
    Cel-shaded vector game icon, one whole crusty oval bread loaf, golden brown.
    Thick heavy black outline, base tone with darker shadow and bright
    highlight, fine crumb texture lines. Pure white background.

    ... wedge of yellow cheese ... fine rind texture lines. Pure white background.
    ... dark green olive fruit ... fine leaf vein lines. Pure white background.

NEGATIVE (shared, _runner/negative.txt) - the anti-mascot half matters most:
    face, eyes, mouth, smile, arms, legs, hands, character, mascot,
    anthropomorphic, creature, sliced, slices, crumbs, multiple objects,
    duplicate, group, pile, text, ... plate, dish, wooden board, cutting board,
    tray, table, surface, wheat, plant, ... grey background, cream background ...


WINNERS
-------
| icon | route | seed | denoise |
|---|---|---|---|
| bread flat | img2img from the rich twin | 1101 | 0.68 |
| bread rich | text to image | 2202 | - |
| cheese flat | text to image | 2202 | - |
| cheese rich | text to image | 2202 | - |
| olive flat | text to image | 1101 | - |
| olive rich | text to image | 1101 | - |

All six then go through the same two mechanical steps:
    python skills/scripts/comfy_cutout.py --out-dir outputs/ex-03/_runner/cut \
      --suffix=-cut --images <the six winners>

    python skills/scripts/icon_finish.py --out-dir outputs/ex-03 --canvas 1024 \
      --fill 0.88 --contour 12 --also-transparent "ex-03-icon-bread-flat-final=..."


THE DECISION THAT SAVED THIS EXERCISE
-------------------------------------
The line weight IS the family, and SDXL gave a different outline thickness on
every run - thin and sketchy more often than not. No wording fixed it. So the
contour was taken away from the model: cut the icon to alpha, dilate that alpha
by exactly 12 px, fill the ring black, put the art back on top. Every icon in the
set now carries an identical contour in absolute pixels and an identical margin,
which is what makes six icons read as one family. Same lesson as the frame
skill: when the trait is geometric and measurable, code beats prompting.

The white background came the same way. SDXL almost never returns a clean white
field from text to image - grey, cream and even a cityscape turned up - so the
background is not argued with either, it is cut and replaced.


WHAT WORKED
-----------
1. Register-by-register, not subject-by-subject. Running all three flats
   together, then all three riches, kept each register internally consistent.
2. One subject clause per subject, reused verbatim in both registers, with only
   the register clause swapped. The pair discipline lives in that reuse.
3. Deriving a flat from its own rich twin by img2img at 0.68 gives both the
   shared silhouette and a genuinely flatter render - the only route that got
   bread's pair to match.
4. The 100px gate is worth the two minutes. All six survive it; the flat row
   stays readable because the contour is heavy and the fields are large.


WHERE THE FAMILY ALMOST BROKE
-----------------------------
5. Prompt length. My first prompts ran past 90 words, and SDXL's CLIP truncates
   at 77 tokens, so "pure white background, no shadow, no text" was silently
   cut off the end of every one. That single bug produced the grey backdrops,
   the decorative frames, the serving boards and the duplicate loaves. Cutting
   the prompts to about 30 words fixed more defects than any negative term did.
6. The refsheet trick backfired. Three flat references pasted into one image and
   bound as an IP-Adapter style anchor at 0.6 imported the reference *objects*:
   wheat stalks grew behind the bread and the olive became a potted plant. At
   0.45 with "style transfer precise" it still bled. For a family of isolated
   objects the reference teaches the model the wrong lesson - it is a content
   source, not a style source.
7. SDXL anthropomorphises lone food items. The olive came back twice as a
   cartoon character with legs and a face until "face, eyes, mouth, arms, legs,
   mascot, anthropomorphic" went into the negative.
8. Bread is the hardest of the three subjects. Its prior pulls hard towards
   bakery scenes, sliced loaves and a serving board; five seeds gave five
   variations of that before img2img from the rich twin settled it.
9. The olive's specular highlight will not leave. Even with "two flat tones
   only, no highlight" the flat olive keeps a white dot, so the register gap on
   the olive is carried by tone count and interior detail rather than by the
   absence of a highlight. This is the one place the flat/rich gap is narrower
   than in the approved pairs.

הערה **Category:** icons. Three new subjects (bread, cheese, olive) x two registers

ex-04 - a six-icon RPG set in the new Hand-Painted Cartoon Fantasy style
========================================================================

ENGINE: FLUX.1-Kontext-dev Q4_K_S on the G5 (192.168.0.27), with all three
style references bound as real pixel conditioning. Cutouts by BiRefNet_HR on the
same box. Panel placement, item scaling and framing by code.

This is the first exercise in the package that does NOT fall back to a cloud
engine, and the reason is a new capability on the G5 - see THE UNLOCK below.


THE UNLOCK: THE REFERENCES ARE NOW PIXELS, NOT ADJECTIVES
---------------------------------------------------------
ex-01 finding 15 listed three things that would close the gap between the local
box and the cloud engines, and one of them was `flux1-kontext-dev`, "which would
let the style card be bound as pixels instead of words". It is now installed:

    flux1-dev-Q4_K_S.gguf
    flux1-kontext-dev-Q4_K_S.gguf     <-- new

So the exercise's instruction 3 - "the three attached images define the STYLE" -
can be obeyed literally for the first time, instead of being translated into
adjectives and hoped for. `comfy_run.py` gained an `--engine kontext` that chains
one `ReferenceLatent` per reference image:

    LoadImage -> (FluxKontextImageScale) -> VAEEncode -> ReferenceLatent
        -> ReferenceLatent -> ReferenceLatent
        -> FluxKontextMultiReferenceLatentMethod(offset)
        -> FluxGuidance -> KSampler

Note what this is NOT. It is not img2img: sampling still starts from an empty
latent, so the references steer *rendering* without dictating *layout*. And it is
not IP-Adapter, which has now imported subjects instead of style on three
consecutive exercises (findings 14, 17, 27). The difference showed up
immediately, on the same prompt and the same seed:

| route | background | brushwork | outline | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SDXL, words only | warm taupe, wrong | flat, cel-like | thin | style missed |
| Kontext, 1 ref @1MP | correct cool slate | crisp painterly | heavy | good, but a drop shadow |
| Kontext, 3 refs @640px | correct cool slate | soft, washed out | thinner | too soft |
| **Kontext, 3 refs @896px** | **correct cool slate** | **crisp painterly** | **heavy** | **locked recipe** |

The SDXL comparison is worth keeping because it is measurable, not just visible.
`icon_set_qc.py` on the four sword attempts:

    sword SDXL words-only   hue  32 deg (warm)   light 336 deg (lower-right)
    sword Kontext 1 ref     hue 218 deg (cool)   light 102 deg (upper-left)
    sword Kontext 3 ref     hue 211 deg (cool)   light  84 deg
    sword Kontext 3 ref 896 hue 212 deg (cool)   light  96 deg

The words-only run is wrong on both axes the brief actually grades - palette and
light direction - and the pixel-bound runs are right on both. Reading the
references and describing them carefully was not a substitute for showing them.


THE LOCKED RECIPE
-----------------
    engine        kontext (flux1-kontext-dev-Q4_K_S.gguf)
    references    all three, resized to 896px long edge, 16-aligned,
                  --no-scale-refs, multi-ref method "offset"
    guidance      2.5      steps 20      cfg 1.0 (fixed by the engine)
    size          1024x1024
    time          ~335 s per render

    python outputs/ex-04/_runner/run-set.py --seeds 1101,2202 --tag k3

Reference resolution is a real dial, not a detail. At 640px the model matched the
references' *softness* as if it were part of the style; at 896px it matched their
brushwork. Three references at 896px cost about 6,500 latent tokens on top of the
output's 4,096, which fits in 8GB and is why they were pre-resized rather than
left to FluxKontextImageScale (three refs at ~1MP each would be ~16k tokens).


THE PROMPTS
-----------
Kontext runs on T5, so the 77-token CLIP ceiling that dominated ex-03 is gone and
the full measured style block fits. Every prompt is head + subject + tail, with
head and tail identical, word for word, across all six:

HEAD (all six, verbatim):
    The attached reference images define the STYLE only: hand-painted cartoon
    fantasy RPG game icon art. Copy their painting style, not their subjects.

TAIL (all six, verbatim):
    Crisp confident edges and high detail. Plain flat dark slate blue-grey
    background, completely empty. No drop shadow, no cast shadow, no ground
    shadow, no glow. A single object, centred, filling most of the frame,
    nothing else in the image, no text.
    ...preceded by the shading clause:
    Thick warm dark-brown outline around the whole silhouette, soft blended
    brush shading inside with visible painterly gradients, rounded glossy
    volume, a soft specular highlight and the light clearly coming from the
    upper left.

SUBJECT CLAUSES (the only thing that changes):
    sword   Draw one upright medieval broadsword: steel blade, ornate gold
            crossguard and pommel, brown leather wrapped grip.
    potion  Draw one round glass potion bottle: glowing blue liquid inside,
            cork stopper, small gold neck band.
    pouch   Draw one brown leather coin pouch tied with a rope drawstring, gold
            coins spilling out at its base.
    map     (rerolled, see below)
    key     (rerolled, see below)
    torch   Draw one wooden torch: brown wooden handle, cloth-wrapped head,
            bright orange flame burning at the top.

Every clause and its evidence in the references is written up in
`_runner/style-block.txt`. Nothing in the style block was guessed: the light
direction came from measuring 38 icons across the three sheets, the palette from
quantising them, the outline weight from full-resolution crops.

NEGATIVE: none in effect. Kontext runs at cfg 1.0, which makes the negative
conditioning inert. `_runner/negative.txt` exists and was used for the SDXL
comparison runs only. This is why every "no" above had to be written into the
POSITIVE prompt, and why two of them still had to be won by rerolling.


WHERE THE MODEL STOPS AND THE CODE STARTS
-----------------------------------------
Six icons on six model-drawn panels would be six different panels. So the panel
was generated once, approved once, and is then placed by code on all six:

    2 panel candidates -> seed 2202 approved (1101 returned a circle, not a
    rounded square) -> BiRefNet cutout -> reused as an anchor, identical pixels,
    on every icon.
